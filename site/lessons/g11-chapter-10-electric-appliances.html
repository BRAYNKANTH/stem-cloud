10.0  Energy conversions in appliances

We use electric energy by converting it into the form we need. An electric appliance is a device that does this: an electric heater, oven and induction cooker give heat; a motor gives kinetic energy; a fluorescent lamp gives light; a radio gives sound; a television gives light and sound.

Figure 10.1: the main energy conversions in some appliances.

In some appliances there is a second conversion: a filament bulb first turns electric energy into heat, and the hot filament then gives light; a fluorescent lamp makes ultraviolet radiation first, which is then turned into visible light.

10.1  Power of an appliance: P = V I

Power is the energy used in a unit time. For an appliance working with a voltage V and drawing a current I, the power is the product of the two. With V in volts and I in amperes, P comes out in watts (W).

Figure 10.2: an appliance with voltage V and current I.
Electric power
P = V I
P in watts (W), V in volts (V), I in amperes (A)
Example 1
A filament bulb on 12 V draws 2 A. What is its power?
P = V I = 12 × 2 = 24 W
Example 2
An electric oven of power 2000 W works on a 230 V supply. What current does it draw?
2000 = 230 × I, so I = 2000 ÷ 230 = 8.69 A

In the heating coil of an oven all the electric energy becomes heat. In some other appliances part of the energy becomes heat because of the internal resistance, and the rest changes into the useful form (light, motion, sound).

10.2  Electric energy used: E = P t

The energy used by an appliance depends on how long it works. If it uses P in each second, the energy used in a time t is P t.

Electric energy
E = P t = V I t
P in W, t in s, E in joules (J)
Example 1
A car head lamp of 50 W is used for 1½ hours. Find the energy used.
E = P t = 50 × 1.5 × 60 × 60 = 270 000 J
Example 2
A 6 V bicycle bulb draws 0.6 A. How much energy does it use in five minutes?
E = V I t = 6 × 0.6 × 5 × 60 = 1080 J

10.3  Efficiency and saving energy

The same job can be done by different appliances. A more efficient one wastes less energy as heat. If 40 % of the energy supplied is lost as heat, only 60 % is useful: the efficiency is 60 %.

Efficiency and bulbs that give the same light.
Light sourcePowerLife time
Filament bulb60 W1200 h
Fluorescent tube22 W3000 h
CFL bulb11 to 13 W8000 h
LED light6 to 8 W50 000 h
  • LED bulbs are the best (lowest power, longest life), but their higher first cost limits their use in Sri Lanka.
  • Cooking and heating: old coil cookers are least efficient. An immersion heater is very efficient for water (all its heat goes into the water). Hot-plate rice cookers lose less heat. Microwave ovens heat inside the food. Induction cookers use a changing magnetic field to heat only the bottom of the pan.
  • LCD (LED) televisions use less power than old cathode ray tube TVs; table fans are more efficient than ceiling fans for cooling a room.
  • Iron all the week's clothes at once, switch off unwanted lights and use CFL or LED bulbs.

10.4  The home electric circuit

Power stations raise the voltage to 132 kV or 220 kV with step-up transformers for the national grid; sub-stations lower it to 33 kV or 11 kV and finally to 230 V, an alternating current of 50 Hz, for houses. The service cable has two wires, the live wire and the neutral wire.

Figures 10.3 and 10.4: electricity entering a house, and the consumer unit.
  • Overload circuit breaker (older houses: service fuse): about 40 A, on the live wire only. If a larger current passes, it disconnects the house. The fuse wire (lead and tin alloy) melts. It belongs to the electricity provider.
  • Electricity meter: counts the energy in kilowatt hours (kW h) for billing. It also belongs to the provider.
  • Isolator (older: main switch with a 30 A fuse): a dual-pole switch that disconnects both live and neutral wires, used for repairs. Everything after the isolator belongs to the consumer.
  • RCCB (trip switch): protects people from electric shock. It cuts the supply automatically when current leaks to the metal casing of an appliance or to earth (about 35 mA), or when more than 30 A flows. It works by electromagnetic induction. Press its test button every few days. It is switched on by raising the lever.
  • Distribution box: sends electricity to lighting circuits (6 A MCB: bulbs and 6 A plugs) and plug circuits (13 A MCB: kitchen heaters, ovens). An MCB (older: fuses of 5 A and 15 A) trips when the circuit current is too large, so only that circuit goes off. MCBs and fuses protect against short circuits and overheated cables only, not against shock. They are always connected on the live wire.
🔴 Why one wire is "live"
  • One of the two supply wires is earthed near the transformer: this neutral wire is at about 0 V. The other wire is at 230 V compared with the earth.
  • A person on the ground touching the live wire has 230 V across the body: 50 mA is a major shock and 100 mA can kill. That is why it is called the "live" wire.
  • This is needed for the RCCB to work: if the live wire touches earth anywhere in the house, the RCCB detects the large current and cuts the supply.
  • Switches turn bulbs on and off one at a time. Plug sockets have live, neutral and earth connections; the larger pin of a three-pin plug is connected to the metal body of the appliance and, through the socket, to the earth wire, so a leak trips the RCCB. Appliances with an insulating plastic cover can use two-pin plugs (no earth).
  • Wires: copper. 1 mm² single cable for 5 to 6 A; seven-strand 1.5 mm² cable for 13 or 15 A plug circuits. Colours: brown live, blue neutral, green earth (older: red live, black neutral).
Figure 10.5: every bulb and socket is in parallel; switches are on the live wire.

Each bulb and plug is connected in parallel. All switches are on the live wire, so touching a bulb holder with the switch off gives no shock. Plug circuits are sometimes ring circuits: each socket gets current through two wires, so thinner cable can be used.

🛟 If the power goes off
  • Turn the overload circuit breaker OFF first, then raise the lever of the RCCB or MCB that tripped, then turn the overload breaker ON. If it trips again, call an electrician.
  • Use only 6 A and 13 A fuses. Do not overload a socket with many appliances through a multi-plug. Put only proper plugs into a socket, never bare wires.
  • Wear rubber slippers or stand on a rubber mat when ironing; use a mat in front of a fridge.
  • Switch off the overload circuit breaker or main switch before changing a bulb in a bathroom. Unplug appliances when not in use.
  • In strong lightning unplug radios and TVs and avoid unnecessary appliances (the RCCB does not protect against lightning).
  • Never use electrical devices with a wet body or touch switches with wet hands. Do not switch on appliances during a power cut. In a fire, switch off the overload circuit breaker. Repairs and extensions only by a trained electrician.

10.5  The kilowatt hour

The joule is small, so electricity bills use the kilowatt hour (kW h), also called a unit: the energy used in one hour by an appliance of power 1 kW. The meter counts it.

Commercial unit
1 kW h = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3 600 000 J = 3.6 × 106 J
number of kW h = (watts ÷ 1000) × number of hours
Example
Four 100 W bulbs are used for 3 hours and five 60 W bulbs for 4 hours each day. How many units in a month (30 days)?
Per day: 100 × 4 × 3 + 60 × 5 × 4 = 1200 + 1200 = 2400 W h. Per month: 2400 × 30 ÷ 1000 = 72 kW h

P = 12 × 2.

I = 2000 ÷ 230 = 8.69.

Textbook example · Ch.10, p.34
A car head lamp has a power of 50 W. Find the energy used when it works for 1½ hours.

E = P t, with t in seconds: 1.5 h = 1.5 × 60 × 60 s = 5400 s.

E = 50 × 5400.

Final answer: 270 000 J.
Textbook example · Ch.10, p.35
A 6 V bicycle bulb draws 0.6 A. How much electric energy is used when it is lit for five minutes?

E = V I t with t = 5 × 60 = 300 s.

E = 6 × 0.6 × 300.

Final answer: 1080 J.
Textbook example · Ch.10, p.47
Four 100 W bulbs are used for 3 hours and five 60 W bulbs for 4 hours daily. Find the electricity used in a month of 30 days.

One day: 100 × 4 × 3 = 1200 W h and 60 × 5 × 4 = 1200 W h.

Total per day = 2400 W h. For 30 days: 2400 × 30 = 72 000 W h.

Divide by 1000 to get kW h.

Final answer: 72 kW h (72 units).

Practice Exercises

Every practice question from the textbook, each with a worked solution

Exercise 10.1Power, energy and the house circuit · p.48–49

1(a).The power of an electric water pump is 750 W. Calculate the current drawn by the motor on a 230 V supply.
P = V I, so I = P ÷ V = 750 ÷ 230 = 3.26 A
1(b).State one type of energy, other than kinetic energy, generated when the motor works.
Heat (also sound), because of the resistance of the coil.
2(a).A flash light bulb is marked 2.5 V and 0.3 A. What is its power?
P = V I = 2.5 × 0.3 = 0.75 W
2(b).If the efficiency of emitting light is 42 %, what happens to the remaining 58 %?
It is lost as heat (the filament and the bulb become hot).
3.Two head lamps of 50 W each and two rear lamps of 10 W each are lit for ½ h. Calculate the electrical energy spent.
Total power = 2 × 50 + 2 × 10 = 120 W; t = ½ h = 1800 s
E = P t = 120 × 1800 = 216 000 J (= 0.06 kW h)
4.The current through a motor cycle bulb on a 12 V battery is 2 A. How much electrical energy is spent if it flows for 15 minutes?
t = 15 × 60 = 900 s
E = V I t = 12 × 2 × 900 = 21 600 J
5(a).Name two instruments used in a domestic circuit for the protection of residents.
The RCCB (trip switch) and the MCB (or fuse).
5(b).Mention the type of protection provided by each.
The RCCB protects people from electric shock: it cuts the supply when current leaks to earth (and when more than 30 A flows). The MCB or fuse protects the circuit from too large a current (overload or short circuit), preventing overheated cables and fire; it does not protect people.
5(c).Explain what is to be done to protect electric equipment in a house when lightning occurs.
Unplug radios and television sets from the sockets and avoid using unessential electrical appliances. (The RCCB does not protect the house circuit from lightning.)
6(a).What is the unit of electrical energy used to make electricity bills?
The kilowatt hour (kW h), called a unit.
6(b).Calculate the energy in joules equal to one commercial unit.
1 kW h = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3 600 000 J (3.6 × 106 J)
6(c).The first 60 units cost Rs. 7.50 each and the next 30 units Rs. 10.00 each. Find the bill for a house using 75 units in a month.
First 60 units: 60 × 7.50 = Rs. 450.00
Next 15 units: 15 × 10.00 = Rs. 150.00
Bill = 450 + 150 = Rs. 600.00
7(a).A 1500 W water heater is used ½ h daily; three 40 W bulbs for 3 h daily; two 60 W bulbs for 2 h daily. Calculate the units used in one day.
Heater: 1.5 kW × 0.5 h = 0.75 kW h
Three 40 W bulbs: 120 W × 3 h = 360 W h = 0.36 kW h
Two 60 W bulbs: 120 W × 2 h = 240 W h = 0.24 kW h
Total = 0.75 + 0.36 + 0.24 = 1.35 units
7(b).Using the rates in 6(c), what is the monthly bill?
Units in 30 days = 1.35 × 30 = 40.5 units (all within the first 60 units)
Bill = 40.5 × 7.50 = Rs. 303.75
8(a).A hot plate or an immersion heater can heat water. Which is more efficient?
The immersion heater.
8(b).Give the reason.
All the heat produced by an immersion heater goes straight into the water. A hot plate also heats the vessel and the air around it, so some heat is lost.
8(c).Why are three-pin plugs used instead of two-pin plugs for immersion heaters?
The third (earth) pin connects the metal body of the heater to the earth wire. If a current leaks to the metal casing, it flows to earth and the RCCB trips, so the user does not get an electric shock.
8(d).When an appliance was switched on, the circuit was disconnected. Give two reasons.
(1) The appliance drew too large a current (overload or a short circuit), so the MCB or fuse tripped. (2) A current leaked through the appliance to earth, so the RCCB tripped.

Chapter Summary & Key Terms

One last read before the exam!

📌 Summary — 7 key points

  1. The power of an electric appliance is the electric energy used per unit time.
  2. Power P = V I (watts); energy E = P t = V I t (joules).
  3. The domestic meter measures energy in kilowatt hours; 1 kW h = 3 600 000 J.
  4. Efficient appliances (LED, CFL, immersion heaters, induction cookers) save energy.
  5. House supply: 230 V, 50 Hz alternating current, through a live and a neutral wire.
  6. The RCCB protects people from shock; MCBs and fuses protect the circuits from large currents.
  7. Bulbs and sockets are in parallel; switches, fuses and MCBs are on the live wire.
