18.1  Work: force that moves something

You already know a force can change the position or shape of an object. When a force makes an object move, we say the force does work. Push the object with a constant 1 N until it moves 1 m along the direction of the force: the work done is defined to be one joule (1 J).

A force of 3 N moves the object 1 m in its own direction: 3 J of work.

With 2 N through 1 m the work is 2 J, with 3 N through 1 m it is 3 J. So the work depends on the force and on the distance moved.

Work done by a force
Work = F × d
F = force (N), d = distance moved in the direction of the force (m), Work in joules (J)
Example 1
What is the work done if a force of 7.5 N moves an object 8 m in the direction of the force?
Work = 7.5 N × 8 m = 60 J
Example 2
The weight of an object is 40 N. What is the work done in lifting it vertically through 2 m?
To lift it we need a force of 40 N upwards. Work = 40 N × 2 m = 80 J
Example 3
The mass of an object is 5 kg. Find the work done in lifting it upwards by 3 m. (g = 10 m s-2)
Weight = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N. Work = 50 N × 3 m = 150 J

18.2  Energy: the ability to do work

Energy is the ability to do work. It is measured in the same unit as work, the joule (J). We need energy for every task, and it comes in many forms.

  • Heat energy
  • Electric energy
  • Magnetic energy
  • Light energy
  • Sound energy
  • Mechanical energy: the kind we study here, in two forms, potential energy and kinetic energy
💡 Work and energy are partners
  • If there is no energy loss, the work done equals the energy used up.
  • Both are measured in joules (J).

18.2a  Kinetic energy: energy of motion

The energy an object has because it is moving is its kinetic energy. Send object A along the floor to hit a light trolley B: the trolley moves some distance and stops. Send A faster: the trolley goes further. Send a heavier A at the same speed: again the trolley goes further. Part of A's kinetic energy is passed to the trolley.

Figure 18.4: A hits the trolley B. More velocity or more mass makes B move further.

So the kinetic energy depends on two factors: velocity and mass.

Kinetic energy
Ek = ½ m v²
m = mass (kg), v = velocity (m s-1), Ek in joules (J)
Example 1
The mass of an object is 6 kg. Calculate its kinetic energy when it moves at 4 m s-1.
Ek = ½ m v² = ½ × 6 × 4² = ½ × 6 × 16 = 48 J
Example 2
Calculate the kinetic energy of a 4 kg object at the instant it moves at 2 m s-1.
Ek = ½ × 4 × 2² = 8 J

18.2b  Potential energy: stored energy

The energy stored in an object because of its position or because its form has changed is its potential energy. When you lift an object you do work on it; that energy is stored in the object as potential energy. Let us find the energy needed to lift 3 kg through 2 m.

Figure 18.5: doing work against gravity. W = 3 kg × 10 = 30 N, work = 30 N × 2 m = 60 J.

Work done = weight × height lifted = m g × h. The work is stored in the object, so at a height of 2 m its potential energy is 60 J. Because the work is done against gravity, it is called gravitational potential energy.

Gravitational potential energy
Ep = m g h
m = mass (kg), g = 10 m s-2, h = vertical height (m)

The potential energy depends on three things: the mass, the gravitational acceleration and the height. Release the object and the stored energy turns back into kinetic energy.

Example 1
Find the gravitational potential energy of a 7.5 kg object at a height of 4 m.
Ep = m g h = 7.5 × 10 × 4 = 300 J
Example 2
A fruit of mass 200 g hangs 4 m above the ground. Find its gravitational potential energy.
200 g = 0.2 kg. Ep = 0.2 × 10 × 4 = 8 J
Figure 18.7: stretching a spring or a rubber band stores elastic potential energy.

When a rubber band or a spring is stretched by a force, its shape changes and the work done is stored in it. This is called elastic potential energy.

18.2c  Energy changes form

A cyclist rides fast along a flat road, then reaches a hill and stops pedalling. The bicycle still climbs, but slows down: its kinetic energy decreases while its potential energy increases. At the top the kinetic energy is smallest and the potential energy is greatest. Rolling down the other side, the potential energy falls and the kinetic energy grows again.

Figure 18.8: kinetic energy turns into potential energy going up, and back again going down.
Figure 18.9: a swing. At A the speed is highest (all kinetic); at B it stops for a moment (all potential).
🔁 Swing from A to B and back
  • A → B: speed falls, height rises: kinetic energy changes into potential energy.
  • At B the velocity is zero: all the kinetic energy has become potential energy.
  • B → A: the potential energy changes back into kinetic energy.
Figure 18.11: a pile driver lifts a weight, then lets it fall.
  • Hydro-electricity: water stored high in a reservoir falls to a lower level. Its potential energy becomes kinetic energy, which turns a turbine to make electricity.
  • Pile and tower: a heavy pile is lifted and released to fall on the soil, packing loose soil before a building's foundation is laid.
  • Sledge hammer: the lifted hammer has potential energy. Released onto a wedge, it hits with a large velocity, passes its kinetic energy to the wedge and splits the wood or stone.

18.3  Power: how fast work is done

The work done in a unit time, the rate of doing work, is called power. If 600 J of work is done in 10 s, the power is 600 J ÷ 10 s = 60 J s-1. One joule per second is one watt (W), so this power is 60 W.

Power
Power = Work done ÷ Time taken   P = W ÷ t
Work in J, time in s, power in watts (W). 1 W = 1 J s-1
Example 1
It takes 10 s to lift a 5 kg mass to a height of 8 m. Find the power.
Weight = 5 × 10 = 50 N. Work = 50 × 8 = 400 J. Power = 400 J ÷ 10 s = 40 W
Example 2
How much work does a machine of power output 100 W do in one minute?
Work = power × time = 100 J s-1 × 60 s = 6000 J

Demonstration: what does kinetic energy depend on?

Textbook · Figure 18.4, p.128

Find out how the speed and the mass of an object change the energy it can pass on.

🧰 You need
  • A small object A, a light trolley B
  • A flat floor, a ruler
📝 Steps
  1. Push A with some speed so that it hits B. Measure how far B moves before it stops.
  2. Push A faster and measure again.
  3. Use bigger and bigger masses for A at the same speed and measure again.
💡 What should you find? (tap)
The trolley goes further when A is faster and when A is heavier. So the kinetic energy depends on both the velocity and the mass.

Activity 18.1: potential energy and height

Textbook · Activity 18.1, p.131

Show that the higher an object is, the more potential energy it has.

🧰 You need
  • Clay (spread about 3 cm thick on the floor)
  • A fairly heavy weight, a ruler
📝 Steps
  1. Release the weight from about 0.5 m above the clay.
  2. Observe the depth of the dent it makes.
  3. Repeat from different heights, with the same part of the weight touching the clay each time.
💡 What should you find? (tap)
The dent gets deeper as the release height increases. The falling weight gave the energy to make the dent, so the potential energy increases with the height.

Activity 18.2: potential energy and mass

Textbook · Activity 18.2, p.132

Show that a heavier object has more potential energy at the same height.

🧰 You need
  • A plastic bottle, sand
  • Clay on the floor, a ruler
📝 Steps
  1. Fill about a quarter of the bottle with sand and let it fall from about 1 m onto the clay. Observe the dent.
  2. Fill it half with sand and release it from the same height.
  3. Fill it completely and release it from the same height.
💡 What should you find? (tap)
The dent gets deeper as the mass increases. So the potential energy increases with the mass.

Worked Examples

Step-by-step solutions to the textbook examples
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.126
What is the work done if a force of 7.5 N acts on an object and displaces it by 8 m in the direction of the force?

Work = force × distance moved in the direction of the force.

Work = 7.5 N × 8 m.

7.5 × 8 = 60.

Final answer: 60 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.126
The weight of an object is 40 N. What is the work done in lifting the object by a vertical distance of 2 m?

To lift the object we must push it up with a force equal to its weight: 40 N.

Work = 40 N × 2 m.

Final answer: 80 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.127
The mass of an object is 5 kg. Find the work done in lifting it upwards by 3 m.

The weight is mg = 5 kg × 10 m s⁻² = 50 N.

The force needed to lift it is 50 N, and the height is 3 m.

Work = 50 N × 3 m = 150 J.

Final answer: 150 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.129
The mass of an object is 6 kg. Calculate its kinetic energy when it moves at 4 m s⁻¹.

Kinetic energy = ½ m v².

v² = 4 × 4 = 16. So Ek = ½ × 6 × 16.

½ × 6 = 3, and 3 × 16 = 48.

Final answer: 48 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.129
Calculate the kinetic energy of an object of mass 4 kg at an instant that it is moving with a velocity of 2 m s⁻¹.

Ek = ½ m v² = ½ × 4 × 2².

2² = 4, so Ek = ½ × 4 × 4 = 8.

Final answer: 8 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.134
Find the gravitational potential energy of an object of mass 7.5 kg at a vertical height of 4 m.

Potential energy = m g h.

= 7.5 kg × 10 m s⁻² × 4 m.

7.5 × 10 = 75 and 75 × 4 = 300.

Final answer: 300 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.134
A fruit on a tree has a mass of 200 g. The vertical height to the fruit from the ground is 4 m. Find the gravitational potential energy of the fruit.

Convert the mass first: 200 g = 200 ÷ 1000 kg = 0.2 kg.

Ep = m g h = 0.2 × 10 × 4.

0.2 × 10 = 2 and 2 × 4 = 8.

Final answer: 8 J.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.136–137
The time taken to lift a mass of 5 kg to a height of 8 m is 10 s. Calculate the rate of doing work (power).

Weight = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N, so the force to lift it is 50 N.

Work = 50 N × 8 m = 400 J.

Power = work ÷ time = 400 J ÷ 10 s = 40 J s⁻¹.

Final answer: 40 W.
Textbook example · Ch.18, p.137
What is the work done in a minute by a machine operating with a power output of 100 W?

Power = 100 W = 100 J s⁻¹. Time = 1 minute = 60 s.

From power = work ÷ time we get work = power × time = 100 J s⁻¹ × 60 s.

Final answer: 6000 J.

Every practice question from the textbook, each with a worked solution

Exercise 18.1Work · p.127 (fill the table)

1.Force 20 N, displacement of force 2 m. Work done = ?
Work = F × d = 20 × 2 = 40 J
2.Displacement 80 cm, work done 24 J. Force = ?
d = 80 cm = 0.8 m
F = work ÷ d = 24 ÷ 0.8 = 30 N
3.Force 15 N, work done 22.5 J. Displacement = ?
d = work ÷ F = 22.5 ÷ 15 = 1.5 m
4.Force 0.75 N, displacement 8 m. Work done = ?
Work = 0.75 × 8 = 6 J

Exercise 18.2Kinetic energy · p.129

1.The mass of a dog is 10 kg. If its kinetic energy is 20 J at a certain instant, what velocity is it running at?
Ek = ½ m v² → 20 = ½ × 10 × v² = 5 v²
v² = 20 ÷ 5 = 4, v = 2 m s-1
2.The kinetic energy of an object of mass 500 g is 9 J when it is moving with a certain velocity. Calculate the velocity at that instant.
m = 500 g = 0.5 kg
9 = ½ × 0.5 × v² = 0.25 v²
v² = 9 ÷ 0.25 = 36, v = 6 m s-1

Miscellaneous exercisesWork, energy and power · p.137–138

1(i).A child lifts a bag of mass 4 kg to a height of 1.5 m. What is the work done? (g = 10 m s⁻²)
Weight = 4 × 10 = 40 N
Work = 40 × 1.5 = 60 J
1(ii).If the child took 3 seconds to do the work above, what is the rate of doing work (power)?
Power = work ÷ time = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 W
2(i).A mass of 800 g is projected vertically upwards at a velocity of 20 m s⁻¹. What is its kinetic energy just as it is projected?
m = 0.8 kg
Ek = ½ m v² = ½ × 0.8 × 20² = 0.4 × 400 = 160 J
2(ii).How long will it take to reach the maximum height?
Going up, the velocity falls by 10 m s-1 every second (g = 10 m s-2), from 20 to 0:
t = v ÷ g = 20 ÷ 10 = 2 s
2(iii).What is the maximum height it reaches?
At the top all the kinetic energy has become potential energy: m g h = 160, so 0.8 × 10 × h = 160.
h = 160 ÷ 8 = 20 m
(Check with the speeds: average speed 10 m s-1 × 2 s = 20 m.)
2(iv).What is its potential energy at the maximum height?
Ep = m g h = 0.8 × 10 × 20 = 160 J
It equals the kinetic energy at the start: no energy was lost.
3(i).A child of mass 35 kg climbs a staircase to a vertical height of 4 m. What is the amount of work he does?
Weight = 35 × 10 = 350 N
Work = 350 × 4 = 1400 J
3(ii).If he took 1 minute to climb the stairs, what is his rate of doing work or power?
t = 1 min = 60 s
Power = 1400 ÷ 60 = 23.3 W (about 23 W)

Chapter Summary & Key Terms

One last read before the exam!

📌 Summary — 9 key points

  1. The work done by a force is the force multiplied by the distance moved in the direction of the force: work = F × d.
  2. If there is no energy loss, the work done equals the energy used up.
  3. Work and energy are measured in joules (J).
  4. The two forms of mechanical energy are potential energy and kinetic energy.
  5. Kinetic energy depends on the mass and the velocity: Ek = ½ m v².
  6. Gravitational potential energy depends on mass, gravitational acceleration and height: Ep = m g h.
  7. The potential energy of an object also changes when its shape changes (elastic potential energy).
  8. When an object moves up, kinetic energy is lost and becomes potential energy; coming down, it changes back.
  9. Power is the rate of doing work: P = W ÷ t. Its unit is the watt (1 W = 1 J s⁻¹).
